Most recruitment agencies are running the world's worst business model — doing all the work upfront with zero guarantee of getting paid. It's a positioning conversation, and it starts in your outreach.

I've worked with and studied dozens of recruitment agencies running cold email campaigns. The ones landing retainers aren't sending better emails — they're sending *different* emails. Let me show you exactly what that looks like.

## Why Contingency Recruiters Lose and Retainer Recruiters Win

Here's the uncomfortable truth: if you're pitching "no placement, no fee," you've already told the prospect you don't believe in your own work enough to charge for it upfront.

Retainer clients aren't just more profitable — they're structurally different. A contingency client treats you like a vendor. A retainer client treats you like a partner. That distinction determines everything: response rates, cooperation on interviews, speed of decision-making, and lifetime value.

The average contingency placement fee is 15-20% of first-year salary. A retained search on the same role is typically 33% split across three payments — and you collect the first third just for taking the engagement. On a $120,000 VP of Sales hire, that's $13,200 in your bank account before you've made a single call.

But here's the counterintuitive part: **retainer clients are actually easier to close via cold email than contingency clients.** Why? Because the type of company willing to pay a retainer already understands the value of specialized recruitment. They've been burned by contingency chaos — three agencies working the same role, candidates getting double-submitted, and zero accountability. Your cold email just needs to speak to that pain.

## The Cold Email Framework That Wins Recruitment Retainers

Forget the generic "I noticed you're hiring" openers. Every recruiter on the planet is sending those. Here's the framework I've seen work consistently for recruiting agencies targeting retainer engagements:

### 1. Lead With a Specific Market Insight, Not Your Services

The worst cold email a recruiter can send:

> *"Hi [Name], I'm a specialized recruiter in the fintech space with 10 years of experience. I'd love to discuss how we can help you fill your open roles..."*

This is a brochure. Nobody reads brochures.

The better approach:

> *"Hi Sarah, fintech SaaS companies in the $10M-$50M ARR range are losing senior engineers to Series C startups at a rate I haven't seen since 2021. The retention problem is becoming a hiring problem — good candidates are getting 4-5 competing offers within 2 weeks of hitting the market. Most hiring managers we talk to aren't seeing this until a role has been open 90 days. Worth a 15-minute call this week?"*

Notice what that email doesn't say: anything about the recruiter's services, fees, or track record. It leads with a market observation that makes the reader feel slightly behind on information they should have. That's the hook.

### 2. Target the Right Companies (This Is Where Most Agencies Fail)

Retainer searches make sense for companies that:
- Have raised a Series B or later (they have budget and urgency)
- Are hiring for VP-level and above roles
- Have had a role open for 60+ days (visible on LinkedIn Jobs)
- Operate in a niche where talent is genuinely scarce

For list building, I pull from:
- LinkedIn Jobs filtered by "posted 60+ days ago" in my target vertical
- Crunchbase for companies that raised $5M+ in the last 12 months
- Job boards like Greenhouse or Lever where you can see the full org's open roles

Once you have your list, run it through a [bulk email verifier](/tools/email-verifier) before launching. I've seen campaigns where 40% of emails were invalid — that's 40% of your sending reputation burning for zero return.

### 3. The Retainer-Specific Sequence Structure

Here's the exact sequence structure I recommend for recruitment agencies targeting retainer engagements:

**Email 1 — The Market Insight Email (Day 1)**
Length: 4-6 sentences. No pitch. Just a specific observation about their talent market that creates mild urgency.

**Email 2 — The Social Proof Nudge (Day 4)**
Length: 3-4 sentences. Reference a specific outcome you delivered for a similar company. No case study links, no attachments. One sentence of proof, one call to action.

> *"Quick follow-up — we recently helped a Series B SaaS company in [their city] fill a VP of Engineering role in 19 days after it had been open for 4 months on their own. Happy to share how we structured that engagement if timing works."*

**Email 3 — The Contrarian Take (Day 9)**
This is the email that gets the most replies. Take a stance that challenges conventional wisdom in their hiring process.

> *"Most companies I talk to are running retained and contingency searches simultaneously thinking it increases their chances. It actually makes it harder — top candidates get double-outreached and the best agencies deprioritize non-exclusive work. If you're working with 3 contingency firms right now, I'd bet money you're seeing this already."*

**Email 4 — The Clean Breakup (Day 16)**
Short. Direct. Creates scarcity.

> *"Going to stop following up after this — I only take on 4-6 retained searches per quarter and I'm evaluating my Q3 pipeline now. If the timing is genuinely off, no hard feelings. If there's a fit, this week works best."*

I've analyzed subject line performance across thousands of recruitment cold emails. Here's what the data shows for retainer-focused outreach:

| Subject Line Type | Average Open Rate | Reply Rate |

The pattern is obvious: specificity wins. The more you demonstrate you've done your homework, the higher your open rates climb. ## Deliverability Is Not Optional for Recruitment Cold Email

Here's where most recruitment agencies self-sabotage. They write decent copy, build a solid list, and then blast it from their main domain using a shared ESP. Within 3 weeks, their domain is flagged, their open rates drop from 35% to 4%, and they conclude "cold email doesn't work for recruitment."

Cold email works fine. Their infrastructure was broken.

The non-negotiables:
- Dedicated sending domains (not your main agency domain)
- Proper SPF, DKIM, and DMARC setup — check yours at the [SPF/DKIM/DMARC checker](/tools/dns-checker)
- Sending volume under 50 emails per inbox per day during warmup

## The Positioning Language That Converts to Retainers

Here's a specific language swap that changes how prospects perceive your offer:

**Instead of:** "We work on a retained basis"
**Say:** "We only take on exclusive searches — it's how we guarantee the quality of our process"

**Instead of:** "Our fee is 33% split into three payments"
**Say:** "The engagement is structured in three phases — research, shortlisting, and close — with a payment aligned to each stage"

**Instead of:** "We'd love to learn more about your hiring needs"
**Say:** "Based on what I'm seeing in your market, I already have a sense of what the search looks like — the call is just to confirm fit before we commit capacity"

The language shift is subtle but the positioning is massive. You're not a vendor waiting to be briefed. You're a specialist deciding whether to take on the engagement.

## What to Do When They Say "We Use Contingency Recruiters"

This objection comes up constantly. Here's how to handle it in a follow-up email:

> *"That's exactly why I reached out. Most of our clients came from contingency relationships that weren't moving fast enough on critical hires. The retained model isn't for every role — honestly, it doesn't make sense below Director level. But for your [specific open role], where you need someone in market confidentially and quickly, the exclusive structure typically cuts time-to-offer by 40-60%. Worth 20 minutes to walk you through how it works?"*

You're not arguing against contingency. You're positioning retained as a specific tool for specific situations. That's a much easier conversation.

## The One Metric That Predicts Retainer Conversion

Stop obsessing over open rates. The metric that predicts whether your cold email campaign will generate retainer clients is **reply-to-meeting conversion rate** — what percentage of people who reply actually book a call.

For recruitment agencies, a healthy benchmark is 25-40% of replies converting to a meeting. If you're below that, the problem is your follow-up speed (reply within 4 hours, always) or your calendar link friction (use a direct Calendly link in every reply, no back-and-forth).

If you're above 40%, you've found a message-market fit worth scaling. Double the list size, not the email volume per contact.
