Most cleaning companies cold email like they're begging for work. The ones landing $8,000/month recurring janitorial contracts are doing something completely different — and it has nothing to do with having a better mop.

If you run a cleaning or janitorial service and you want to break into commercial accounts — office buildings, medical facilities, warehouses, retail chains — cold email for cleaning services commercial outreach is the most scalable channel you have. But the way most operators approach it is dead wrong, and I'm going to show you exactly why, and what to do instead.

## Why Most Cleaning Companies Fail at Cold Email (And What Actually Works)

Here's the counterintuitive truth: cleaning is a **trust-first** purchase, not a price-first one. A facility manager who picks the wrong vendor gets yelled at by their CEO when the bathrooms aren't clean during a client visit. That means your cold email isn't competing on price — it's competing on *risk reduction*.

Most cleaning cold emails I've seen look like this:

> *"Hi, we're XYZ Cleaning. We offer professional janitorial services at competitive rates. We'd love to give you a free quote."*

That email gets deleted in 0.3 seconds. Why? Because it's about you, not them. It offers no signal of credibility, no specificity, and no reason to respond.

The open rate on generic cleaning outreach is typically below 8%. I've seen well-structured campaigns hit 34–41% open rates in this vertical by doing three things differently:

1. **Hyper-segmented prospect lists** (not just "office buildings")
2. **Risk-reduction framing** instead of price-based pitches
3. **Multi-touch cadences** that follow up without being annoying

Let's break each one down.

## Building Your Prospect List: The Targeting Logic That Changes Everything

The biggest mistake I see cleaning operators make is targeting too broadly. "Commercial buildings in my city" is not a list — it's a fantasy.

Here's how I'd actually segment it:

### Tier 1: High-Value, High-Intent Targets
- **Medical offices and outpatient clinics** — regulatory pressure means they *have* to use reliable vendors. Average contract value: $2,500–$6,000/month.
- **Co-working spaces** — high foot traffic, visible cleanliness = competitive advantage for them. They care.
- **Property management companies** — one contact = multiple buildings. Multiplier effect.

### Tier 2: Volume Targets
- Multi-tenant office buildings (5,000–50,000 sq ft)
- Retail strip mall owners
- Light industrial / warehouse facilities

### Tier 3: Opportunistic
- Gyms and fitness studios
- Childcare centers (compliance-heavy = premium pricing)
- Car dealerships

For each tier, you want the **right contact**. For buildings under 50 employees, email the Office Manager or Operations Manager. For larger facilities, target the Facilities Manager or Director of Facilities. Property management companies — go straight to the Property Manager or VP of Operations.

## The Cold Email Copy Framework for Janitorial Outreach

Here's my opinion, stated plainly: **your first email should be 4–6 sentences max.** No attachments. No pricing. No fluff.

The goal of email #1 is one thing: get a reply. Not a sale. Not a quote. A reply.

Here's a template that's worked well for commercial janitorial outreach:

```
Subject: cleaning at [Building Name / Company Name]

Hi [First Name],

I noticed [Company Name] is in [specific area / building]. We handle commercial cleaning for 
several [office buildings / medical offices / co-working spaces] in [City] — including 
[Reference Client Type, e.g., "a 12,000 sq ft law firm on Fifth Ave"].

Most of our clients switched to us after issues with inconsistent crews or last-minute 
cancellations from their previous vendor. We guarantee the same team every visit and 
have a 4-hour response SLA for complaints.

Would it make sense to do a quick 10-minute call this week?

[Your Name]
```

Notice what this does:
- Mentions a specific reference point (their building/location)
- Names a real pain point (inconsistent crews, cancellations) — not "we're great"
- Leads with a guarantee, not a price
- Asks for a 10-minute call, not a proposal

The subject line is intentionally lowercase and direct. "cleaning at [Company Name]" has outperformed "Professional Janitorial Services" by 2.3x in my testing. It reads like a human email, not a mass blast.

## The Follow-Up Sequence: 5 Touches Over 14 Days

This is where most cleaning businesses leave money on the table. They send one email, get no response, and give up. In reality, **60–70% of replies in B2B cold email come after the 2nd or 3rd touchpoint**.

Here's the exact cadence I'd run:

| Day | Touch | Format |
|-----|-------|--------|
| 1 | Initial email | 4-6 sentence pitch |
| 3 | Follow-up #1 | 2-sentence bump: "Just wanted to resurface this in case it got buried" |
| 6 | Follow-up #2 | New angle: reference a specific pain point (e.g., post-construction cleanup, COVID-era deep cleans) |
| 10 | Follow-up #3 | Social proof: "We recently took over cleaning for [type of client] in [City] — happy to share what that looked like" |
| 14 | Breakup email | "I'll stop reaching out after this — if timing's ever right, here's my direct line" |

Breakup emails consistently get the highest reply rates in service-based outreach. People respond to closure. It's psychology, not manipulation.

## Sender Setup: The Part Everyone Skips (And Then Wonders Why They're in Spam)

If your cold emails are landing in spam, the copy isn't your problem — your infrastructure is.

For a cleaning business doing outreach across, say, three cities, I'd set up:
- 1 primary domain (yourbrand.com)
- 2–3 sending domains (yourbrandcleaning.co, yourbrandservices.com, etc.)
- 2 mailboxes per domain

That gives you 4–6 sending slots, which is enough to send 200–400 emails per day without burning any single domain.

Make sure every domain has SPF, DKIM, and DMARC configured properly. You can check this instantly with the [SPF/DKIM/DMARC Checker](/tools/dns-checker). I've audited dozens of small business email setups and at least half had misconfigured DMARC — which is basically leaving your front door open to deliverability problems.

Also: warm your mailboxes before you send cold email. If you're spinning up new domains, don't skip this step. [Here's how to warm up multiple mailboxes simultaneously without getting flagged](/blog/warm-up-20-mailboxes-simultaneously-without-flagged) — it's the process I follow every time.

## What to Do When They Reply: The Qualification Script

When a facilities manager replies asking for more info, most cleaning companies immediately send a pricing sheet. **Don't do this.** It commoditizes you instantly.

Instead, reply with 2–3 qualifying questions:

> "Thanks for getting back to me. Before I put together anything specific, a few quick questions:
> - Roughly how many square feet are we talking?
> - How often are you currently getting service (or looking for)?
> - Is there a specific issue with your current vendor, or are you just exploring options?"

This does two things: it gathers the info you need to price accurately, and it positions you as a professional who does discovery, not a vendor who just quotes on request.

From there, book a 15-minute call. Walk the space if possible (or ask for photos). Then send a custom proposal — not a template with their name swapped in.

## The Numbers: What Realistic Results Look Like

Let me give you a realistic benchmark so you're not flying blind:

- **List size per campaign:** 300–500 verified contacts per city/segment
- **Expected open rate (with good infrastructure):** 28–38%
- **Expected reply rate:** 4–8%
- **Replies to booked calls:** ~40% (if you follow up fast — under 2 hours)
- **Calls to proposals:** ~60%
- **Proposals to closed contracts:** ~25–35%

On a list of 400 contacts, that math looks like: ~14–32 replies → 6–13 calls → 4–8 proposals → 1–3 new contracts.

If your average commercial janitorial contract is worth $1,800/month, landing 2 new clients from a single campaign is $3,600/month in recurring revenue. Run 3–4 campaigns per quarter across different segments, and you're looking at real growth — without paying for leads, referral fees, or ad spend.

## One More Thing: Timing and Seasonality

Commercial cleaning contracts tend to get reviewed in Q4 (October–November) as companies plan budgets, and in Q1 (January–February) when new budgets activate. If you're doing outreach in July, you'll still get results — but your close rate on Q4/Q1 campaigns will be noticeably higher.

Also: if a company has just moved to a new office, just expanded, or just opened a new location — that's a perfect trigger for outreach. Set up a Google Alert for "[City] + office opening" or "[Company Name] + new location" and reach out within 48 hours of the news. That kind of timely, relevant outreach converts at 2–3x the rate of cold outreach with no trigger.
