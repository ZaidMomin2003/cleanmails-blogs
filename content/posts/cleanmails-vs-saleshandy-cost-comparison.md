Most people do the Cleanmails vs Saleshandy cost comparison wrong. They look at the monthly sticker price, shrug, and pick whichever tool has the shinier UI. Run the real numbers out to 12 months — factoring in seat limits, email validation credits, add-on costs, and infrastructure — and the story changes dramatically.

I'm going to do that math for you right now, with actual numbers from real plans as of mid-2025. No affiliate links. No soft-pedaling. Just a brutally honest breakdown of what you'll actually spend.

## The Baseline: What You're Actually Comparing

Before we get into the Cleanmails vs Saleshandy cost comparison properly, let's define the use case. I'm assuming:

- **5 sender accounts** (a reasonable starting point for any serious outreach)
- **10,000 emails/month** sent volume
- **Email validation** for your lists (non-negotiable if you care about deliverability)
- **Cadences/sequences** with at least 3 steps
- One person managing everything

This isn't a power user scenario. This is a solo founder, a small SDR team, or an agency managing a handful of clients. Normal stuff.

## Saleshandy: What You Actually Pay

Saleshandy's pricing looks reasonable on the surface. Their Outreach Starter plan runs around **$36/month**. But here's where it gets interesting.

### The Hidden Cost Stack

**Sender accounts:** Saleshandy's base plans cap you on connected email accounts. To get to 5 senders on a plan that also gives you meaningful send volume, you're realistically looking at their **Outreach Pro plan at $99/month** (billed monthly) or $79/month if you commit annually.

Let's use $79/month (annual commitment, so you're locked in).

**Email validation:** Saleshandy has built-in verification, but the credits are limited on base plans. If you're pulling fresh lists regularly — and you should be — you'll burn through those fast. Expect to either upgrade or buy add-on credits. Conservative estimate: **$20-30/month** in verification costs if you're validating 5,000-10,000 contacts/month.

**Prospect limits:** Here's the one that catches people off guard. Saleshandy caps the number of prospects you can upload by plan. On Outreach Pro you get 100,000 prospects total, which sounds like a lot until you're running multi-client campaigns or doing serious list cycling. At that point you're bumping up to **Outreach Scale at $149/month**.

**SMTP:** Saleshandy connects to your existing email provider. That means you still need Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 accounts for each sender. At $6/mailbox/month for Google Workspace, that's another **$30/month for 5 senders**.

### Saleshandy 12-Month Real Cost

| Line Item | Monthly | 12-Month Total |
|---|---|---|
| Saleshandy Outreach Pro (annual) | $79 | $948 |
| Email validation add-ons | $25 | $300 |
| Google Workspace (5 mailboxes) | $30 | $360 |
| **Total** | **$134** | **$1,608** |

And that's being conservative. If you upgrade to Scale tier, add more senders, or pay month-to-month, you're looking at **$2,000-$2,400/year** easily.

## The Contrarian Take: Subscription Tools Are a Trap for High-Volume Senders

Here's something the cold email tool industry doesn't want you to think about: **subscription pricing is specifically designed to be painful to leave**.

When you're 18 months into Saleshandy, you've got campaigns, templates, sequences, and reporting all locked inside their platform. Migrating is a project. So you keep paying. Every month. Even when the pricing goes up (and it always goes up — Saleshandy has adjusted pricing multiple times).

I wrote more about this dynamic in [Why Monthly Cold Email Subscriptions Are Killing Your ROI](/blog/why-monthly-cold-email-subscriptions-are-killing-your-roi) — but the short version is: subscription tools make financial sense for vendors, not buyers. The break-even math almost always favors a one-time purchase within 6-8 months.

At $497 one-time, Cleanmails breaks even against Saleshandy's $134/month real cost in **under 4 months**.

## Where Saleshandy Genuinely Wins

I'm not going to pretend Saleshandy is a bad tool. It isn't. Here's where it legitimately has an edge:

**1. Setup speed.** Saleshandy is cloud-hosted SaaS. You're sending within 20 minutes of signup. Self-hosted tools require a VPS, a deployment, and domain configuration. If you need to send *today* and you've never touched a server, Saleshandy is faster to start.

**2. Support and hand-holding.** If you want someone to blame when things break, SaaS support exists. With self-hosted, you're more on your own (though the Cleanmails community and docs are solid).

**3. Integrations ecosystem.** Saleshandy has more native integrations out of the box. If your stack is heavily CRM-dependent and you're not comfortable with [Zapier-based workarounds](/blog/zapier-cold-email-automation-comparison), that matters.

But here's my honest take: if you're sending more than 3,000 emails/month and you've been doing cold email for more than 90 days, the setup friction of a self-hosted tool is a one-time 2-hour investment that pays back for years.

## The Deliverability Variable Nobody Talks About

Both tools are only as good as your sending infrastructure. This is where most cost comparisons fall apart — they ignore deliverability failure costs.

If your emails land in spam, your **effective CPL (cost per lead) goes through the roof** regardless of which tool you're using. A $79/month tool with 15% inbox rate is more expensive than a $6/month VPS with 85% inbox rate.

The deliverability levers that actually matter:

1. **Proper authentication** — SPF, DKIM, and DMARC. Use the [SPF/DKIM/DMARC Checker](/tools/dns-checker) to audit your domains before sending anything.
2. **List hygiene** — Validate every list before uploading. The [Bulk Email Verifier](/tools/email-verifier) catches bad addresses that would otherwise tank your sender score.
3. **Sender rotation** — Spreading volume across multiple senders is the single highest-leverage deliverability tactic at scale. I broke down exactly why in [Why Unlimited Sender Rotation Changes Everything for High-Volume Outreach](/blog/unlimited-sender-rotation-benefits-high-volume-outreach).
4. **SMTP setup** — Cleanmails' inbuilt SMTP means you control the sending infrastructure end-to-end. With Saleshandy, you're routing through Google/Microsoft, which has its own reputation considerations. Read [Why I Stopped Using Google Workspace for Cold Email](/blog/why-i-stopped-using-google-workspace-cold-email) if you want to understand why that matters more than most people realize.

## A Real Scenario: Agency Running 3 Client Campaigns

Let's make this concrete. You run a small lead gen agency. Three clients, each wanting ~3,000 emails/month sent from 2 dedicated sender addresses per client. That's **6 senders, 9,000 emails/month**.

**Saleshandy cost:**
- You need Outreach Scale ($149/month) to handle 6 senders and the prospect volume
- Plus 6 Google Workspace mailboxes at $6/each = $36/month
- Plus validation credits = ~$30/month
- **Total: ~$215/month = $2,580/year**

**Cleanmails cost:**
- $497 one-time license
- One slightly beefier VPS (~$12/month for Hetzner CX31)
- **Total year one: $641. Total year two: $144.**

The agency scenario is where self-hosted crushes SaaS. No per-seat pricing. No per-client upsells. You add a client, you add a sender — no invoice change.

## My Verdict

Saleshandy is a well-built tool. If you're just getting started, testing cold email as a channel, or need to be live in an hour, it's a reasonable choice. The UX is clean and the onboarding is fast.

But if you've been doing cold email for more than a few months, you're sending at any real volume, or you're running campaigns for multiple clients — the Cleanmails vs Saleshandy cost comparison isn't close. You're paying $1,600-$2,500/year for Saleshandy versus $569 for Cleanmails in year one, and the gap widens every single year after that.

The subscription model is a comfort tax. You pay it for convenience and hand-holding. Once you've done this long enough to not need that — and it doesn't take long — you're just leaving money on the table.
